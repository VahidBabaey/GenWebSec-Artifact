# Attack-agent prompts (withheld)

**The attack-agent prompts are intentionally not included in this repository, and
will not be added.**

## Why

The attack-agent prompts instruct a language model to generate SQL-injection and
cross-site-scripting payloads that both exploit a target application and evade a
deployed ModSecurity/OWASP-CRS web application firewall. Releasing them would
amount to publishing operational guidance for producing WAF-bypassing attacks.

This omission is a deliberate dual-use safeguard. It is not missing
documentation, and it is not an incomplete artifact.

## What is provided instead

The paper describes the attack agent in enough detail to interpret every reported
result:

- its role in the co-evolutionary loop,
- its generation parameters (batch size, the temperature allocation across
  requests, deduplication),
- the execution-based validity oracle that confirms a payload actually exploits
  the application, and
- the WAF-replay step that confirms a bypass.

The structured outcomes of the attack agent are released as data, without the
raw payloads: per-context and per-seed counts, bypass rates, and aggregate
statistics live under `results/`. Raw generated bypass payloads are not released;
see `SECURITY.md`.

## What is deliberately absent

To make sure the withheld prompts cannot be reconstructed indirectly, the
following are also kept out of the public artifact:

- attack-prompt system or user text, in whole or in part,
- attack-prompt template variables or scaffolding,
- backup or variant copies of the attack prompts, and
- run logs or result files whose fields would let the prompts be rebuilt.

The defense-agent prompts, which are released under `../defense/`, were checked to
ensure they do not embed the attack prompts; concrete payload examples in them
have been redacted.
